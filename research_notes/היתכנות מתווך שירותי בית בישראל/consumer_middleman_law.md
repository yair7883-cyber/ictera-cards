## 1. Is it misleading (הטעיה) to present a one-person brokerage as a service company without disclosing that all work is subcontracted? (חוק הגנת הצרכן, התשמ"א-1981, s.2)

### Takeaway
Section 2(a) bans any act or omission that is *liable* to mislead a consumer (עלול להטעות) on a material matter (עניין מהותי). The statute's non-exhaustive list reportedly names, as a separate item, the identity of the manufacturer, importer or **service provider** (נותן השירות), cited in case law as s.2(a)(6). So it is a real risk to pretend to be the company that performs the work (for example "our team", "our certified installers", a fleet or equipment the business does not own) when an unrelated independent performs it. Simply not mentioning that the business subcontracts is a greyer area: liability for an omission turns on whether the fact is material to the deal.

### Cited Findings
- s.2(a) prohibits a business from doing anything, by act or omission, in writing, orally or in any other way, that is liable to mislead a consumer on a material matter. The word "liable" means actual deception need not be proven. Knowingly withholding a material detail can be misleading by omission. [S] — [Martindale IL](https://www.martindale.co.il/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F/)
- Case law quoted by Dinrega lists "the identity of the manufacturer, importer or service provider (s.2(a)(6))" among the statutory material matters. Another ruling discussed misleading under s.2(a)(1) and (7) as also giving a right to cancel the transaction. [S, quoting rulings] — [Dinrega](https://www.dinrega.com/gs/%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%94%20%D7%91%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%94%D7%98%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%99%D7%AA%20%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F%20%D7%9C%D7%92%D7%91%D7%99%20%D7%96%D7%94%D7%95%D7%AA%20%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9F)
- The list of material matters is non-exhaustive. Secondary sources disagree on how many items it has (one says 22). [S] — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F); [israel-law.co](https://israel-law.co/%D7%AA%D7%97%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%99-%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A7/%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%94/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F/)
- The Consumer Protection Authority (CPA) treats reviews by people who never used the service, or who do not exist, as misleading (see §5). This shows the Authority applies s.2 to presentation that makes a business look established. [S] — [Davar](https://www.davar1.co.il/273545/)

### Inferences
- Highest risk: claims that are actually false, such as "we employ N gardeners", "our technicians", photos of a crew or vehicles that do not belong to the business, "established in 20XX" when it is new, or presenting a subcontractor as a staff employee. Each of these is a misrepresentation about the service provider's identity or characteristics, which is a listed material matter.
- Lower but real risk: not mentioning subcontracting while making no false statement. Whether the identity of the performing worker is "material" depends on the facts. It is more likely to be material where the customer pays for specific expertise (water-softener installation tied to plumbing work or a warranty) than for routine garden maintenance.
- Practical mitigation (inference): state in the terms and quote that the work is performed by independent professionals the company selects and supervises, and name the performing professional on the order or work confirmation. This also supports the s.4ב identity-disclosure duty (see §2).
- In parallel to consumer law, the Contracts Law (General Part) 1973 s.15 (misrepresentation, including non-disclosure) can let the customer rescind. That section number comes from the internal reference (no URL) and was not verified this session.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full current text of s.2(a). The numbering of the "service-provider identity" item (s.2(a)(6)) comes from a case quote on a secondary site. Verify on Nevo.
- I found no Israeli ruling or CPA decision that deals specifically with a broker presenting itself as the performing company. This is a genuine gap, not a finding that such conduct is safe.

## 2. Identity details a business must disclose (name, address, ID / company number) in ads, on a website, and in phone or online sales

### Takeaway
At least two statutory duties exist:
- **Distance sales:** before the deal, the business must give its name, ID or corporate number and address, plus the main features and price of the service. Afterwards it must deliver a written disclosure document (s.14ג(b)).
- **s.4ב:** the business's name and ID number must appear on order forms, warranty certificates, installation confirmations and correspondence addressed to a specific consumer.

I could not verify a general duty to print ID numbers in every advertisement.

### Cited Findings
- In distance marketing, the business must disclose its name, identity number and address (in Israel and abroad), together with the main characteristics of the asset or service and its price. [S] — [Nadel law](http://nadel-law.co.il/Index.asp?ArticleID=657&CategoryID=291&Page=1). Note: one search summary attributed this to "s.4א" and another to "17ג". By my reading it sits in s.14ג(a), but the number is **not verified**.
- The business must give the consumer a written disclosure document under s.14ג(b), no later than the time of supply. Until it is delivered, the cancellation clock does not start (see §3). [S] — [jpath](https://jpath.co.il/consumer-protection-law-section-14g/)
- s.4ב (per one version found): the business must disclose its name and ID number on an order form, warranty certificate, installation confirmation and correspondence addressed to a particular consumer, and on its website in relation to its telephone service. [P via snippet] — [Wikisource, Consumer Protection Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F); [tamc.co.il text of the law](https://tamc.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/3.pdf)
- A lawyer's guide (2026) advises that an e-commerce site show the full name, business number (ח.פ / ע.מ) and business address. This is advice, not statutory text. [S] — [ziko.co.il](https://www.ziko.co.il/blog/hganat-hatzarchan-mischar-2026)
- One law-firm reproduction of the statute contains a clause stating that "the provisions of this section also apply to advertising", and the statutory definition of advertising includes sponsored content. I could not tell which subsection it belongs to. [S] — [Frenkel & Co.](https://www.ranfrenkel.com/article_195/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F,-%D7%94%D7%AA%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%90--1981)

### Inferences
- For a middleman who sells by phone, WhatsApp or web form, almost every sale is a distance sale. So the business must give its legal identity (the owner's name and ID number, or a company name and number) before or at the deal, and send a written disclosure document. A brand name alone is not enough.
- If the business is an עוסק מורשה trading under a brand, the disclosure must show the legal person behind the brand. This works against any wish to look like a larger company.
- If the subcontractor issues the order form or installation confirmation (the broker model), s.4ב falls on the subcontractor. The broker's own website and ads still carry its own disclosure duties.

### Gaps
- The exact section and subsection numbers for the identity duties (14ג(a), 4ב) and any ad-specific identity rule were not verified against the full text. Check Nevo s.4ב, s.7, s.14ג and the Consumer Protection (Disclosure) regulations.
- No source found on a duty to identify the business at the start of an outbound marketing call. Check s.14ג and CPA guidance.

## 3. Distance sales (עסקת מכר מרחוק) and door-to-door sales (עסקת רוכלות): cancellation rights, deadlines and fees for services

### Takeaway
- **Distance sale of a service:** the customer may cancel within 14 days from the transaction or from receiving the disclosure document, whichever is later. Secondary sources say cancellation must also come at least 2 days (described as "not rest days" in one source and "business days" in another) before the service is due to start.
- **Elderly (65+), disabled and new immigrants:** 4 months where the deal involved a phone call or chat.
- **Door-to-door (רוכלות, any place other than the business premises, for example a quote signed in the customer's garden):** 14 days for services from the agreement or from receipt of the contract, whichever is later; 4 months for the same protected groups.
- **Cancellation fee** when there is no defect: the lower of 5% of the price or NIS 100. If the cancellation is due to a defect, misrepresentation or non-conformity, no fee may be charged.

### Cited Findings
- Distance service: 14 days from the transaction or from receipt of the disclosure document, whichever is later (s.14ג(c)(2)). [S] — [jpath](https://jpath.co.il/consumer-protection-law-section-14g/)
- Service cancellation must be made at least two days that are not rest days before the service starts. This applies to courses, subscriptions and services with a future start date. A 2026 article on the same site words it as "at least 2 business days". This is a terminology conflict and must be checked in the text. [S] — [Bizportal 20036709](https://www.bizportal.co.il/financialconsumerism/news/article/20036709); [Bizportal 20042624](https://www.bizportal.co.il/financialconsumerism/news/article/20042624)
- Exceptions to distance cancellation (s.14ג(d)) include perishable goods, goods made to the consumer's order, information products, and hospitality, travel, vacation or entertainment services due within 7 non-rest days of the deal. Cancellation may be given by fax or electronic communication. [S] — [jpath](https://jpath.co.il/consumer-protection-law-section-14g/); [ez-suit](https://ez-suit.org/articles/cancel-within-14-days)
- Cancellation fee under the Consumer Protection (Cancellation of Transaction) Regulations: 5% of the price of the goods or service, or NIS 100, whichever is lower. [P via Kol Zchut snippet] — [Kol Zchut, דמי ביטול](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%99_%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%9C)
- No cancellation fee where the cancellation is due to a defect (s.14ה(d), per a secondary source). [S] — [jpath](https://jpath.co.il/consumer-protection-law-section-14g/)
- Extended period (s.14ג1): 4 months for a senior citizen (65+), a person with a disability or a new immigrant, where the deal included a phone call or chat. The fee cap stays at 5% or NIS 100. [P via snippet] — [Kol Zchut, disability page](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%94_%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%90%D7%95_%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%AA_%D7%9E%D7%9B%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%A2%D7%9C_%D7%99%D7%93%D7%99_%D7%90%D7%93%D7%9D_%D7%A2%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%92%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA)
- Door-to-door sale (עסקה ברוכלות) means a deal made anywhere other than the business's own premises. Cancellation is within 14 calendar days of receiving the product or the contract. For a service, the period runs from the agreement or from receipt of the contract, whichever is later. Continuing services may be cancelled after they start, within the period. 4 months for disabled persons, seniors (65+) and new immigrants. [P via snippet] — [Kol Zchut, ביטול עסקה ברוכלות](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%94_%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA); [Kol Zchut, definition](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%94_%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA)
- Kol Zchut notes that these Israeli cancellation rules apply to Israeli sites. [P via snippet] — [Kol Zchut, ביטול עסקת מכר מרחוק](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%AA_%D7%9E%D7%9B%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7)
- 2026 small-claims example: a distance-sale cancellation in which a bride's deposit to a make-up artist had to be refunded. [S] — [Kashty law, 2026 ruling summary](https://kashtylaw.com/blog/psika-consumer-protection-2026-006)

### Inferences
- The middleman's whole sales funnel (ad, then call or WhatsApp, then quote) creates distance sales. An on-site quote signed at the customer's home creates door-to-door sales. In both, customers can cancel cheaply (at most NIS 100) for 14 days. Seniors, a large share of garden and water-softener customers, have 4 months.
- Subcontractors should not be booked or paid irrevocably before the cancellation window closes, and the start date should be at least two non-rest days after the deal. Otherwise the middleman bears the cost of the subcontractor's cancelled slot. This is an operational inference.
- If the disclosure document is never sent, the 14-day clock never starts. A cheap, systematic paperwork failure therefore turns into an open-ended cancellation right.

### Gaps
- Exact text of s.14ג(c)(2) and whether the "2 days before service" rule is in the statute or the regulations, and whether it says "not rest days" or "business days".
- Regulation title and year: Consumer Protection (Cancellation of Transaction) Regulations, 2010. The Hebrew year (התשע"א) is from memory and not verified.
- Whether a gardening subscription or periodic maintenance contract is an "עסקה מתמשכת" with additional rules (s.13א or 13ד). Not researched for lack of access. Check Nevo.

## 4. Price display and written contracts for services

### Takeaway
Consumer law requires a "total price" (מחיר כולל) that includes VAT and every mandatory payment. The Minister may set specific price-display rules for service providers. Breaching price-display duties can bring exemplary damages without proof of harm, up to NIS 10,000 (NIS 50,000 for repeated or continuing breaches, per secondary sources). I found no general statutory duty to put a home-service contract in writing outside the distance and door-to-door rules. A long-running "renovation warranty" bill would require written contracts and a one-year warranty, but its status is unclear and it is probably not enacted.

### Cited Findings
- Amendment 11 (2002) added the price-display chapter. "Total price" includes all payments for the asset or service and all taxes, including VAT, fees and mandatory payments (s.17א). [P] — [Knesset, חוק הגנת הצרכן (תיקון מס' 11), התשס"ב-2002](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/15/law/15_lsr_300479.pdf)
- s.17ג (per the same document, partly garbled): the Minister may set rules under which a business whose occupation is providing a service shows the total price only, in Israeli currency. [P, text partly garbled] — [Knesset PDF](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/15/law/15_lsr_300479.pdf)
- Exemplary damages (פיצויים לדוגמה, s.31א) without proof of harm: up to NIS 10,000, and NIS 50,000 for a repeated or continuing breach or aggravated circumstances. [S] — [Bizportal 2026 consumer-compensation calculator](https://www.bizportal.co.il/additives/424/consumer-auto-comp.html); [Protocol](https://protocol.co.il/introducing-service-price/)
- Renovation-warranty bill: proposed versions require a written contract and a warranty of at least one year (thresholds reported as NIS 20,000 or NIS 47,000). One article says there is currently no rule requiring a renovation contract to be in writing. The sources conflict on the bill's status (one article says it was "approved this week", another that it passed only a preliminary reading, an opinion piece that it "fails time after time"). Dates of these articles are unclear, possibly years old. [S] — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3751382,00.html); [Maariv opinion](https://www.maariv.co.il/business/consumerism/Article-976891)

### Inferences
- Quotes, ads and the website should show final prices including VAT (18%, per the internal reference; a tax researcher covers it), or prices "from X including VAT". Showing pre-VAT prices to consumers is a classic enforcement and class-action trigger.
- In practice a written quote or contract is needed anyway: the s.14ג(b) disclosure document for distance deals, and the door-to-door contract that starts the cancellation period. A written scope of work is also the main defence in small-claims disputes (see §11).

### Gaps
- Exact current text of s.17ב, s.17ג and s.31א (amounts, and which breaches qualify) and whether service-price display regulations exist that cover gardening or installation. Check Nevo and CPA guidance.
- Current status of the renovation-warranty bill (Knesset legislation database).

## 5. Enforcement: administrative fines (עיצום כספי) by the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority

### Takeaway
The CPA can impose administrative fines per breach. Amounts are index-linked and differ by section and by whether the business is a corporation. One secondary snapshot of the statute text shows 2026-adjusted base amounts such as NIS 25,950 (corporation) or NIS 8,260 (non-corporation) for one class of breaches, and NIS 53,070 or NIS 29,490 for another, with a 2026 cap of NIS 10,586,720 per type of goods or service. These figures come from a single search summary and are **not verified** against the official adjustment notice. Fines are published (corporations for 4 years, individuals for 2 years) and must be paid within 45 days.

### Cited Findings
- 2026-adjusted amounts as reported from the Wikisource text: NIS 53,070 (corporation) / NIS 29,490 (non-corporation) for one category, and NIS 25,950 / NIS 8,260 for others. Cap per type of goods or service is NIS 10,586,720 for 2026. Aggravated circumstances reportedly allow 1.5 times the base. Publication: 4 years for a corporation, 2 years for an individual. Payment within 45 days. [P via search summary; **not verified**] — [Wikisource, Consumer Protection Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F)
- Worked example in a 4 Nov 2026 law-firm article: base fine of NIS 25,950 per breach, NIS 51,900 in total, cut by 20% to NIS 41,520. A follow-up exact-phrase search for "25,950" did not confirm the figure independently. [S] — [Orel law office, 4.11.2026](https://orellawoffice.co.il/2026/11/04/%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%9B%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%99-%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F/)
- The CPA may impose a fine for **each** breach of the listed provisions (s.22ג per the summary), so the number of breaches multiplies the total. A law-firm update discusses how a "unit of breach" is defined for this purpose. [S] — [Arnon Tadmor-Levy update](https://arnontl.com/he/news/haganat-hatzarchan/)
- Older press figures (about NIS 22,530 for a corporation and about NIS 7,000 for an individual) are outdated. [S] — [ynet (older article)](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/by0016f6ske)
- Government payment service for CPA fines. [P] — [gov.il](https://www.gov.il/he/service/consumer_protection_and_fair_trade_financial_sanctions)
- Recent enforcement examples: a fine of over NIS 1.2 million on an electronics company for misleading consumers (4 May 2026) [S] — [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3835289); a direct-sales company's fine reportedly cut on appeal from about NIS 1.1 million to NIS 61,898 [S] — [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/1096416)
- A State Comptroller report of June 2026 covers consumer-protection issues. An investigative piece citing the Comptroller says the CPA inspects only about 0.5% of businesses. [P] — [State Comptroller 2026 economic report, ch. 108](https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Documents/2026/Economic-Report/2026-Economic-Report-108.pdf); [S] — [Mako investigation](https://www.mako.co.il/news-money/consumer/Article-993a43653fa7d81027.htm)

### Inferences
- For a one-person business, the realistic consumer-law exposure is more likely private: small claims, exemplary damages under s.31א, and (while still available) class actions. CPA fines are less likely, given the CPA's limited inspection coverage. One CPA fine for a repeated practice (for example, no disclosure document on every distance sale) would still be several tens of thousands of shekels.

### Gaps
- Official 2026 adjustment notice for the fine amounts, and the mapping of each amount to the sections a middleman is likely to breach (s.2, s.14ג, s.17ב). Check gov.il CPA pages and Nevo s.22 to s.22ט.
- Criminal penalties (s.23) not researched.

## 6. Fake or incentivised reviews and "looks like a legitimate business" presentation

### Takeaway
The CPA's guideline on online reviews (draft published 5 January 2021; final status not confirmed) treats as misleading under s.2:
- fake reviews (by people who do not exist or never used the service),
- editing, removing or diluting genuine reviews (except illegal content or terms-of-use breaches),
- suppressing negative reviews, or rewarding positive ones without transparency.

Incentives are allowed only if they do not depend on the review being positive, and they must be disclosed. Influencer endorsements count as advertising and need disclosure of the commercial link.

### Cited Findings
- The CPA published a draft guideline on online reviews and ratings on 5 January 2021. [P] — [gov.il draft supervisor guideline (PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/news/cpfta_internetopinion2021/he/docs_draft_supervisor_guide.pdf)
- Three categories of misleading reviews: (1) fake reviews, by non-existent people or people who did not use the product, including through bloggers and influencers; (2) changing, removing or diluting reviews, except illegal or terms-breaching content; (3) not publishing negative reviews, or promising an incentive for a positive review without transparency. An endorsement by a celebrity or influencer is advertising and requires disclosure of the commercial link. [S] — [Davar](https://www.davar1.co.il/273545/); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/SyzEMeG0w)
- Incentives for reviews may be offered if not conditioned on the review's content. The guideline recommends a reporting mechanism for fake reviews, publishing reviews in full with the date of writing and of use, and disclosing the review publication and removal policy. [S] — [Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%A2%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%95%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A8/); [Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/1857/117554/)
- Status conflict: a Chamber of Commerce page says the final disclosure text was updated at the Chamber's request, while a ynet piece described the rules as taking effect within three months and open to comment. I found no confirmed date for a final version. [S] — [Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/1857/117554/); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/SyzEMeG0w)
- Influencers advertising products or services must disclose the commercial link (CPA position). [S] — [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/news-money/consumer/Article-29b42937dedda71027.htm); [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/826514)

### Inferences
- A new brand without a track record that seeds its site or listings with reviews written by the owner, friends or subcontractors ("happy customer, Ra'anana"), or that shows subcontractors' past jobs as "our projects" without saying so, is squarely within the CPA's "fake review" and s.2 categories.
- Lawful route (inference): collect real reviews from real customers. A neutral incentive (for example a discount for any review) is acceptable if disclosed. Show subcontractors' portfolios only as "work by professionals in our network".

### Gaps
- The final text and date of the CPA guideline, and whether the CPA has fined anyone specifically for fake reviews. ## 7. Standard-form contracts (חוק החוזים האחידים, התשמ"ג-1982): can the middleman shift liability to the subcontractor or limit its liability?

### Takeaway
Two kinds of clause are presumed unfair (תנאי מקפח), and the supplier bears the burden of rebutting the presumption:
- A clause exempting the supplier fully or partly from liability it would otherwise bear by law (s.4(1)).
- Per secondary sources, a clause letting the supplier transfer performance of its obligations to a third party.

An exemption from liability for bodily injury or malice is void outright. The general unfairness test in s.3 applies on top of the presumptions. So a "general contractor" whose customer terms say "we are not liable for the subcontractor's work, claim against him" is very likely unenforceable.

### Cited Findings
- s.4(1): "a condition that exempts the supplier, fully or partly, from liability that would have been imposed on it by law but for that condition" is presumed unfair until proven otherwise. The presumption can be rebutted by showing the clause is reasonable and fair, and the burden is on the supplier. [S] — [Fridman](https://www.fridmanwork.com/MDx-lawyer36468.html); [Meiri](https://meiri.co/blog/limitation-of-liability); [YBD law](https://www.yosefbdlaw.com/news/%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%A4%D7%97%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%90%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93/)
- A clause in a standard contract exempting the supplier from liability for bodily injury or malicious acts is void. The section number was not shown in the snippet. [S] — [Meiri, standard contracts](https://meiri.co/law/standard-contracts)
- Section 4 lists categories of presumptively unfair terms, including exemption from liability, an unreasonable right to cancel or change the contract, and transfer of liability or performance to a third party. One source gives the example of a travel company passing performance to another company. The exact subsection number for the third-party clause was **not verified**. [S] — [Fridman](https://www.fridmanwork.com/MDx-lawyer36468.html); [israel-law.co](https://israel-law.co/%D7%AA%D7%97%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%99-%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A7/%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%94/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%94%D7%90%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- The s.4 presumptions do not displace the general s.3 test of unfairness. [S] — [YBD law](https://www.yosefbdlaw.com/news/%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%A4%D7%97%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%90%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93/); concept page: [Kol Zchut, חוזה אחיד](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%94_%D7%90%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93)

### Inferences
- **General-contractor model:** clauses such as "the company acts only as coordinator and bears no liability for defects or damage caused by the contractor", "the company may replace the contractor at its discretion", or a cap on liability at the fee paid are all presumptively unfair in a consumer standard contract. A court in a dispute can strike them out, and a class action or the Standard Contracts Tribunal can attack them systemically.
- **Broker model:** a clause disclaiming liability for work the broker never undertook to perform is not an "exemption from liability that would otherwise be imposed by law". It reflects the real allocation of obligations. It is much more defensible, provided the structure is genuine and clearly disclosed (see §12).
- Interpretation: by the internal reference (no URL), Amendment 3 to the Contracts Law (General Part) (third reading 5.1.2026) keeps standard-form and non-business contracts on an intent-based reading even for contracts made from 7.1.2026. A consumer will not be bound by a literal reading of fine print. Not verified this session.

### Gaps
- Full text of s.4 (all subsections and numbering) and of the section voiding exemptions for bodily injury or malice. Check Nevo.
- No case found applying s.4 specifically to a home-services platform or broker.

## 8. Liability for defects, delays and damage: חוק חוזה קבלנות, התשל"ד-1974 and the independent-contractor rule in פקודת הנזיקין [נוסח חדש] (s.15)

### Takeaway
- **Contract:** the Contractor Contract Law covers any contract to do work or provide a service for pay where the contractor is not the client's employee. Its rules are default rules (s.8(a)). A middleman who contracts with the customer to deliver "garden landscaping" or "water-softener installation" is the contractor toward the customer. The law contains no provision letting the main contractor escape liability because it used a subcontractor; commentary treats subcontracting as affecting only the internal relationship. The law gives the customer a notice-and-repair mechanism: notify the defect, give a chance to repair, otherwise repair at the contractor's cost or deduct from the fee.
- **Tort:** s.15 generally exempts a person who engages an independent contractor from liability for torts committed in the work. The exceptions include negligence in choosing the contractor (s.15(1)) and authorising or ratifying the act (s.15(3)).

### Cited Findings
- s.1: a contractor contract is "a contract to do work or to provide a service for pay, where the contractor is not the employee of the client". It covers any self-employed person's service contract with clients, not only builders. [P/S] — [Nevo, חוק חוזה קבלנות](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/71886.htm); [workrights.co.il](https://www.workrights.co.il/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA)
- s.8(a): the law's provisions apply where no other law has special provisions and no different intention is implied by the parties' agreement, so they are default rules. [S] — [din.co.il](https://www.din.co.il/articles/2813/B-7587/); [Mishpati](https://www.mishpati.co.il/article/law-contracting)
- I found no explicit statutory provision on subcontracting. Eyal Zamir's commentary on the contracts laws has chapters on the main contractor's liability toward the client and on how subcontracting affects the client and main-contractor relationship. [S] — [Nevo, Zamir commentary ToC](https://www.nevo.co.il/Content/Safrut/TableOfContents/zamir-contract/)
- Defect mechanism, per a reproduced text of the law:
  - If the contractor does not repair within a reasonable time after the client's notice, the client may repair and exercise the powers under s.3(a).
  - A defect whose repair is urgent may be repaired without first giving the contractor the usual opportunity.
  - If the defect cannot be repaired, the client may deduct an appropriate sum from the fee.
  - s.5 gives the contractor a lien on property the client handed over for the work.
  - **Section-level numbering was not verified.** The internal reference places the notice duty in s.3. [S, garbled extraction] — [Toledano, text of the law](https://www.toledano.co.il/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%AA%D7%A9%D7%9C%D7%93-1974/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- Torts Ordinance s.15: a person who contracts with an independent contractor to do specific work is not liable for a tort arising in the course of the work, except in the cases listed in the section. Case law extended the principle to persons engaging contractors for construction. [S] — [Mako legal guide, 2026 Q1](https://www.mako.co.il/news-law_guide/2026_q1/Article-f4269ab13f8dc91026.htm); [Fridman, vicarious liability](https://www.fridmanwork.com/LAWx-lawyers127860.html)
- Exceptions: s.15(1), negligence in selecting the contractor; s.15(3), the employer "authorised or ratified" the act, which a 2026 ruling applied to impose full liability for property damage in construction works. [S] — [Mako 2026](https://www.mako.co.il/news-law_guide/2026_q1/Article-f4269ab13f8dc91026.htm)
- Academic view: liability under s.15 is the principal's personal liability, not vicarious liability, and the case law is inconsistent. [S, academic] — [HUJI, "Issues in tort law: personal liability for others' torts (ss.12 and 15)"](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%92%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%A0%D7%96%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9F-%D7%90%D7%97%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%96%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%AA-%D7%A1%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%9D-12-%D7%95-15-%D7%9C%D7%A4%D7%A7/)
- Supreme Court, רע"א 1326/18 סמארט קלאב אחזקות בע"מ נ' בתיה כהן: discussed whether a principal company can shelter behind the independent-contractor defence, and an active duty of supervision where the principal authorised or ratified the act. [P] — [Supreme Court decision PDF](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/Home/Download?path=HebrewVerdicts\18\260\013\g05&fileName=18013260.G05&type=4); [Afik commentary](https://he.afiklaw.com/caselaw/11763/8)
- Renovation damage as tort claims, a typical home-service dispute pattern. [S] — [GCO](https://gco.co.il/tort-articles/when-renovation-turns-into-lawsuit/)

### Inferences
- **General-contractor model:** toward the customer, the middleman is the contractor under the Contractor Contract Law for defects, non-conformity and delay, whoever physically did the work. Its recourse is only against the subcontractor, under their own contract. In tort, the middleman can try s.15 for third-party or property damage caused by the subcontractor's negligence. But (a) negligent selection (for example an unlicensed or uninsured installer, or no checks) and (b) directing or approving how the work is done (setting the method, the materials or the timetable) can defeat the defence. Toward its own customer, the contractual claim usually makes s.15 irrelevant anyway.
- **Broker model:** if the subcontractor genuinely contracts and invoices directly, the subcontractor is the "contractor" toward the customer. The broker's exposure narrows to its own representations (s.2 consumer law, misrepresentation), negligent referral (a weak basis; unverified), and its own data or marketing conduct. A court may still look at substance over form: who priced the job, who the customer dealt with, whose brand appeared on the van and invoice.
- Water-softener installation probably involves the supply of goods as well as work (supply and installation). The Sale Law and product-liability rules may then also apply. Not researched.

### Gaps
- Full text and numbering of Contractor Contract Law ss.2 to 4 (performance standard, notice duty, repair, materials) and Torts Ordinance s.15 (all exceptions, including any for illegal, dangerous or non-delegable work). Check Nevo: https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/71886.htm and the Torts Ordinance on Nevo.
- No case found on a lead-generation brand or broker being held liable as a contractor.

## 9. Is a licence needed to broker services? Is there a duty to disclose the commission?

### Takeaway
I found no licensing regime for brokering home services in general. The licence requirement for brokers applies to **real-estate** brokerage, defined as bringing parties together for a transaction in a right in land. An unlicensed real-estate broker is not entitled to fees. I found no statutory duty, specific to home-service brokers, to disclose the commission amount to the customer. Disclosure duties come through s.2 (identity of the service provider) and the distance-sale disclosures.

### Cited Findings
- The Real Estate Brokers Law prohibits anyone without a valid licence from engaging in real-estate brokerage, and an unlicensed broker is not entitled to brokerage fees (subject to a Magistrate ruling applying a "flexible approach" in an exceptional case). [S] — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001337160); licence service [P] — [gov.il realtor licence](https://www.gov.il/he/service/realtor_license)
- s.14(a) sets cumulative conditions for a fee: a valid licence, compliance with s.9 (written order) and being the "efficient cause". "Brokerage" is defined as bringing parties together for pay to enter a transaction in a right in land. [S] — [Klikat Nadlan](https://klikatnadlan.co.il/buyersgroupsbrokerslaw/); [Toledano, text of the law](https://www.toledano.co.il/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%A8%D7%A7%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9F-%D7%94%D7%AA%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%95-1996/%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%95%D7%95%D7%9A-%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%9D/)

### Inferences
- Brokering gardening or water-softener jobs falls outside the Real Estate Brokers Law, since the transaction is a service contract, not a right in land. No broker licence appears to be needed. Whether the **work itself** needs a licence or registration (for example plumbing-related installation, or a contractor's registration for development works) is a different question, covered by the researcher on "regulation of the services".
- No express commission-disclosure duty was found. However, if the broker presents itself as "independent advice" or a "neutral comparison" while being paid by the subcontractor, the concealed payment could be a s.2 misrepresentation. Neutral wording ("we connect you with a professional from our network") avoids that.

### Gaps
- No source confirms the absence of a general services-broker licence. This is an inference from finding none. Check with the Ministry of Economy and the CPA.
- No ruling or guidance found on commission disclosure for platforms or lead brokers under Israeli consumer law.

## 10. Privacy: Privacy Protection Law after Amendment 13 (in force 14 August 2025) for a small lead and customer database shared with subcontractors

### Takeaway
After Amendment 13, **database registration** is required only for:
- public bodies, and
- data brokers whose main purpose is passing data to others for consideration, holding data on more than 10,000 people.

A database with special-sensitivity data on more than 100,000 people must be **notified** to the Privacy Protection Authority (PPA) within 30 days. A small home-services middleman therefore almost certainly needs **no registration**, but every database owner still bears the duties of notice at collection (expanded by Amendment 13), purpose limitation and data security. Under the Data Security Regulations 2017, a small customer database is most likely "basic" level, or "managed by an individual" if run by one person with few users. Sharing data with subcontractors is outsourcing and engages the outsourcing rules (reg. 15). Private remedies include damages without proof of harm: up to NIS 10,000 under the new s.15A, and up to NIS 50,000 under s.29A (per secondary sources).

### Cited Findings
- Amendment 13 in force 14.8.2025. Registration remains only for databases of public bodies (except a database of the body's own employees) and of data brokers whose main purpose is transferring data to others for consideration, with data on more than 10,000 people. Data on more than 100,000 people with special sensitivity, in a database not subject to registration, must be notified to the PPA within 30 days. Previously registered databases stay on the register until the controller notifies that registration no longer applies. [S] — [SEC-IT FAQ](https://sec-it.co.il/tikun-13-faq/); [ALAW guide](https://alaw.co.il/privacy/law/amendment-13/); [Oz, Lotan et al.](https://o-n.law/privacy-protection-law-amendment-no-13/); [govextra.gov.il](https://govextra.gov.il/privacy-protection-regulations/privacy-protection-regulations/)
- Exemption from registration is not an exemption from the law's other duties on collection, management, processing and security of personal data. Amendment 13 expanded the duty to inform at collection. [S] — [SEC-IT FAQ](https://sec-it.co.il/tikun-13-faq/); [Tzemach Schneider](https://zes.co.il/%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A1-13-%D7%9C%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA/)
- Data Security Regulations, 2017 (תקנות הגנת הפרטיות (אבטחת מידע), התשע"ז-2017): published in Kovetz HaTakanot 7809 on 8.5.2017, in force one year later.
  - Four levels: managed by an individual, basic, medium, high.
  - **Basic** applies to a database not managed by an individual and not in medium or high.
  - **Medium** includes databases whose main purpose is collecting data to pass to others, and databases with sensitive data (medical, biometric, criminal record and so on).
  - **High** means medium with 100,000+ people or more than 100 authorised users.
  - Medium and high require an internal or external audit at least every 24 months.
  - Regulation 15 deals with outsourcing.
  - Sources differ on the exact medium-level threshold.
  [P/S] — [Nevo, regulations](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/144811.htm); [PrivacyCheck](https://privacycheck.co.il/guides/data-security-levels); [SEC-IT, levels](https://sec-it.co.il/security-levels/); [ALAW, regulations guide](https://alaw.co.il/privacy/security/regulations/)
- Damages: Amendment 13 empowers courts to award up to NIS 10,000 without proof of harm (s.15A). Under s.29A, up to NIS 50,000 without proof of harm. [S] — [Razi](https://razi.co.il/blog/%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9F-13-%D7%9C%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA/); [Hakimovich](https://law-yh.co.il/a-privacy-amendment13.html); [ALAW](https://alaw.co.il/privacy/law/amendment-13/)
- Administrative fines under Amendment 13: secondary sources conflict. One cites a cap of 5% of annual turnover, with amounts doubled for databases of more than 1 million data subjects. Another cites up to NIS 2.3 million or 2% of turnover. A blog's "NIS 800,000 for a 200,000-customer database" is a hypothetical. [S, conflicting] — [Seven law](https://seven.law/articles/privacy-amendment-13); [STRA](https://stra.co.il/blog/amendment-13-to-the-privacy-protection-law/); [NELAW](https://nelawcenter.com/amendment-13-to-the-privacy-protection-law/)
- Small businesses are covered: Amendment 13 applies to any business holding a customer database, including small ones. [S] — [SimplyClub](https://simplyclub.co.il/%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%A8-13-%D7%9C%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7-%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F.html); [iCount](https://www.icount.co.il/blog/amendment-13/)

### Inferences
- A database of a few hundred to a few thousand leads and customers (name, phone, address, job details) needs no registration and has no sensitive data, so it is likely "basic", or "managed by an individual" if the owner alone has access. The duties are: a written collection notice on web forms and WhatsApp intake (purpose, whether providing data is mandatory, who receives it, including "independent service providers who will perform the work"), a minimal security procedure, access control, and a written data-handling agreement with each subcontractor under reg. 15.
- Watch-out: if the middleman sells or passes leads to subcontractors **for consideration** as its main business (pure lead-selling, which is the broker model), it edges toward the "data broker" definition. Registration would then follow once it holds data on more than 10,000 people, and the database would also move to medium security level ("main purpose is passing data to others"). That is a structural reason to keep the business framed as service sales, not lead sales.
- Contact data reused for marketing also triggers the spam law (see §11). The two regimes run in parallel.

### Gaps
- The exact text of s.11 (notice duty) after Amendment 13 and the PPA's official fine tariff. Check the PPA's Amendment-13 guide at https://www.gov.il/he/departments/the_privacy_protection_authority.
- Conflicting reports on the first Amendment-13 fine. One secondary search summary attributed a NIS 256,000 fine to Meuhedet health fund (21.7.2026). The internal reference cites NIS 256,000 on Menora Mivtachim (22.10.2026), reduced 60% from NIS 640,000, for late breach reporting. Verify on the PPA's own notices before citing either.
- Whether s.29A amounts are index-linked, and whether double damages require intent to harm (internal reference says yes; not verified this session).

## 11. Marketing messages: חוק התקשורת (בזק ושידורים), התשמ"ב-1982, s.30A (spam law)

### Takeaway
Advertising messages (email, SMS and other electronic messages; WhatsApp is treated the same in practice) need the recipient's **prior explicit consent** (s.30א(b)). The main exception is for existing customers (s.30א(c)), whose conditions are cumulative:
- the recipient gave their details during a purchase or negotiation,
- was told they would be used for advertising,
- was given a chance to refuse and did not, and
- the ad concerns similar products or services.

Every message must allow refusal and be marked as advertising. A court may award **exemplary damages of up to NIS 1,000 per message** without proof of damage where sent knowingly. Under רע"א 1954/14 חזני נ' הנגבי (4.8.2014) the full NIS 1,000 is the starting point per message, reducible in suitable cases.

A class-action reform approved in committee on 15.10.2026 would exempt businesses with turnover of NIS 9 million or less from class actions. For others it would require prior notice and 60 days to cure before a spam class action. Final enactment is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- s.30א(b): an advertiser may not send an advertisement without the recipient's prior explicit consent. Exemplary damages of up to NIS 1,000 per message, not dependent on damage, where sent knowingly. [S] — [Y-Law](https://www.y-law.co.il/blog/spam/spam-damages/); [Kravits](https://www.kravits-law.com/post/%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%90%D7%9D-%D7%96%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99-%D7%A2%D7%93-1-000-%D7%A9-%D7%97); [Shai Erez](https://shaierez.co.il/hok-haspam-laasakim/)
- Supreme Court רע"א 1954/14 חזני נ' הנגבי (4.8.2014): the starting point is the full NIS 1,000 per message, reducible in appropriate cases. The Magistrate court had awarded only NIS 1,000 for 27 emails across 13 mailboxes. [S] — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3638500,00.html); [Toledano case page](https://www.toledano.co.il/%D7%A8%D7%A2%D7%90-1954-14-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%9F-%D7%97%D7%96%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%93-%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%91%D7%99/%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%94-%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%9D/)

- s.30א(d): the recipient may refuse in writing or in the way the message arrived. s.30א(e): the ad must be clearly and prominently marked as advertising. [S] — [Kravits](https://www.kravits-law.com/post/%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%90%D7%9D-%D7%96%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99-%D7%A2%D7%93-1-000-%D7%A9-%D7%97); [SystemCompare](https://systemcompare.co.il/israel-spam-law/)
- WhatsApp broadcasts are lawful only to recipients who gave prior written consent, or to existing customers under s.30א(c). [S] — [PowerAds](https://powerads.ai/blog/whatsapp-broadcast-spam-law)
- Ministry of Communications spam FAQ. [P] — [gov.il](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/17052018_7)
- Class-action reform:
  - The joint Constitution and Economy committee approved, on 15.10.2026, for second and third reading, an amendment exempting businesses with annual turnover of NIS 9 million or less (index-linked) from the Class Actions Law.
  - For five causes, including the spam law, it would require prior notice to the business and 60 days to cure, which bars a class action if the breach is cured.
  - Committee approval is not law. No report of a plenum vote was found. The Chamber of Commerce mentions a special session on 16.9.2026 during the election recess.
  [S] — [TheMarker 15.10.2026](https://www.themarker.com/news/politics/2026-10-15/ty-article/000001a1-3eb7-d19d-adfd-bfb73ad50000); [Davar](https://www.davar1.co.il/702151/); [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/takdin/news/article/20044834); [Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/1858/164123/)
- One marketing site cites a criminal fine of "up to NIS 226,000" for spam. This is single-source and not verified. [S, unverified] — [ziko.co.il](https://ziko.co.il/blog/hok-spam-2026)

### Inferences
- **Leads** who filled in a quote form are in "negotiation". Marketing to them is lawful only if the form told them their details would be used for advertising and gave a chance to refuse (an unticked opt-out box or explicit opt-in), and only for similar services. Bought lead lists, or numbers scraped from groups, require explicit consent. Without it, each message carries roughly NIS 1,000 of small-claims exposure, and serial spam plaintiffs exist.
- Transactional messages (appointment confirmations, quotes the customer asked for) are not advertising. Mixing promotions into them makes them advertising.
- Even if the class-action exemption becomes law, it does not stop **individual** small-claims spam suits, which are the main risk for a micro-business.

- No data found on the number of spam class actions against small home-service businesses specifically.

## 12. Trade names and trademarks for the brand; risk of names similar to existing companies

### Takeaway
A brand name gets statutory protection in Israel by registering a trademark with the Israel Patent Office under the Trade Marks Ordinance. Registration covers Israel only and only the registered classes. A purely descriptive name ("Sharon Gardening", "Soft Water Israel") is likely to be refused for lack of distinctiveness. Using a name confusingly similar to an existing business exposes the middleman to a passing-off claim (גניבת עין) under s.1 of the Commercial Torts Law, 1999, including injunctions, even without any registration by the other party.

### Cited Findings
- The main framework is the Trade Marks Ordinance, alongside the Commercial Torts Law and unjust enrichment. Statutory protection requires registration with the Registrar of Trade Marks at the Israel Patent Office. Protection is territorial and limited to the registered goods or services classes. [S] — [Drori Stav IP](https://dwo.co.il/he/%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%97%D7%A8-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C/); [Hebrew Wikipedia, סימן מסחר](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%9F_%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%97%D7%A8)
- Examination covers distinctiveness (not generic or merely descriptive). Accepted marks are published for opposition. Refusals can be appealed to the District Court. Examination takes about 1 to 1.5 years from filing. [S] — [Drori Stav IP](https://dwo.co.il/he/%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%97%D7%A8-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C/)
- Fees (2026, secondary): NIS 1,904 for one mark in one class, NIS 1,432 for each additional class, and NIS 893 per class for the fast track. Older sources show NIS 1,654 (January 2022). Registration lasts 10 years and is renewable. [S] — [Ettorney, fees](https://www.ettorney.co.il/%D7%97%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%92/%D7%9B%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%9C%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%97%D7%A8%D7%99/)
- Passing off (גניבת עין) is under s.1 of the Commercial Torts Law, 1999: a business causing its goods or services to be mistaken for another's, or as connected with it. Example: a Supreme Court appeal arising from a Karshi v. Gift Center claim based on s.1, with an injunction. Another example is a Magistrate case about competing baking-camp ads mistaken for the plaintiff's. [S] — [HaMichlol, גניבת עין](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%91%D7%AA_%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9F); [Toledano, ע"א 1898/12 case page](https://www.toledano.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%90-1898-12-%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%96-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-2006-%D7%91%D7%A2%D7%9E-%D7%95%D7%90%D7%97-%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%93-%D7%A7%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%99-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0/%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%94-%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%9D/)

### Inferences
- A one-person business can trade under a brand without registering anything, but then has no exclusive right to it. A coined or distinctive name plus a trademark application in the relevant services classes (gardening and landscaping; installation and maintenance of water-treatment equipment) is the way to own the brand. Fees run about NIS 1,900 to 3,300 for one or two classes, excluding attorney fees (secondary figures).
- Before choosing a name: search the Israel Patent Office trademark database, the Registrar of Companies, and Google and social names in the same niche. A name like an established water-softener company's can also be misleading under consumer-law s.2, since it misleads about the service provider's identity.
- If the business incorporates (חברה בע"מ), the company name is registered with the Registrar of Companies, but that is not a trademark right.

### Gaps
- Whether there is any registry or requirement for a sole trader's (עוסק מורשה) trade name in Israel. Not found. Check the Tax Authority and Corporations Authority pages.
- Statutory compensation without proof of damage under the Commercial Torts Law (reportedly s.13), and its amount: **not verified**.
- Official 2026 trademark fee schedule: check the Israel Patent Office on gov.il.

## 13. Small-claims court exposure (בית משפט לתביעות קטנות)

### Takeaway
The small-claims ceiling is **NIS 39,900 from 1 January 2026** (index-linked). The filing fee is about 1% of the claim, and parties normally appear without lawyers. Typical home-service disputes are a technician arriving late or not at all, charging after cancellation, overcharging, refunding deposits on cancelled distance deals, poor or non-conforming work, and property damage during works. Spam suits at up to NIS 1,000 per message are a separate, common category.

### Cited Findings
- Ceiling NIS 39,900 from 1.1.2026 (י"ב בטבת התשפ"ו), following the index change. Ordinary people can file without a lawyer. [P via snippet] — [Kol Zchut, הגשת תביעה קטנה](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A9%D7%AA_%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%94_%D7%A7%D7%98%D7%A0%D7%94); [gov.il filing service](https://www.gov.il/he/service/filing_a_small_claim)
- Fee about 1% of the claim amount. [S] — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20025353)
- Typical consumer claims are reflected in a 2026 compensation calculator covering a late technician, charging after cancellation and overcharging. [S] — [Bizportal calculator](https://www.bizportal.co.il/additives/424/consumer-auto-comp.html)
- Example 2026 rulings: refund of a deposit after a distance-sale cancellation [S] — [Kashty law](https://kashtylaw.com/blog/psika-consumer-protection-2026-006); property damage in building works and liability through the independent contractor rule [S] — [Mako 2026](https://www.mako.co.il/news-law_guide/2026_q1/Article-f4269ab13f8dc91026.htm)

### Inferences
- In the **general-contractor** model, the customer sues the brand (the middleman) for anything up to NIS 39,900: defects, delays, damage, refunds. The middleman must then recover separately from the subcontractor, by third-party notice or a separate claim. Because companies cannot file small claims, a middleman that incorporates cannot use small claims against its subcontractor.
- In the **broker** model the customer's natural defendant is the subcontractor. Customers often sue both, so the broker should expect to be named and to have to show it was not a party to the work contract.
- Under the internal reference (Courts Law ss.60, 63, 64; no URL): only individuals may file; a lawyer appears only with leave; appeal only by leave. **Not verified this session.**

## 14. Comparison of structures: (a) general contractor vs (b) referral / broker

### Takeaway
- **(a) General contractor:** the middleman is legally the service provider and contractor toward the customer. It carries full defect, delay and damage liability, consumer-law duties (disclosure, cancellation, price display, identity) and small-claims exposure. Its standard-form terms cannot validly push that liability onto the subcontractor. The upside is a single, honest customer relationship, full control of pricing and margin, and a legitimate claim to "be" the service company.
- **(b) Broker:** liability for the work moves to the subcontractor, who contracts, invoices and carries the Contractor Contract Law duties. But this holds only if the structure is genuine and transparent. Presenting as the service company while using a broker structure combines the s.2 misleading risk with the s.4 standard-contracts risk. It also pushes the business toward "data broker" privacy classification if lead-selling is its main purpose.

### Cited Findings
- Contractor Contract Law covers anyone contracting to perform work or a service for pay. Its rules are default rules (s.1, s.8(a)). [P/S] — [Nevo](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/71886.htm); [din.co.il](https://www.din.co.il/articles/2813/B-7587/)
- Clauses exempting the supplier from liability imposed by law are presumed unfair in standard contracts (s.4(1)). Clauses transferring performance or liability to a third party are listed among presumptively unfair categories (subsection not verified). [S] — [Fridman](https://www.fridmanwork.com/MDx-lawyer36468.html)
- Identity of the service provider is a listed material matter for misleading (s.2(a)(6), per a case quote). [S] — [Dinrega](https://www.dinrega.com/gs/%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%94%20%D7%91%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%94%D7%98%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%99%D7%AA%20%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F%20%D7%9C%D7%92%D7%91%D7%99%20%D7%96%D7%94%D7%95%D7%AA%20%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9F)
- Torts Ordinance s.15 shields a principal from an independent contractor's torts, except for negligent selection (15(1)) and authorisation or ratification (15(3)). [S] — [Mako 2026](https://www.mako.co.il/news-law_guide/2026_q1/Article-f4269ab13f8dc91026.htm)
- Data brokers holding data on more than 10,000 people must register their database under Amendment 13. Databases mainly for passing data to others are at "medium" security level. [S] — [SEC-IT](https://sec-it.co.il/tikun-13-faq/); [PrivacyCheck](https://privacycheck.co.il/guides/data-security-levels)

### Inferences (comparison table; all rows are inferences from the findings above)

| Issue | (a) General contractor (middleman signs and invoices) | (b) Referral / broker (subcontractor signs and invoices, pays fee) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the "contractor" / service provider toward the customer | Middleman | Subcontractor |
| Defects, delay, non-conformity (Contractor Contract Law) | Middleman fully liable, with recourse only against the subcontractor | Subcontractor; middleman exposed mainly for its own representations |
| Third-party or property damage (tort) | Contract claim by the customer anyway. s.15 defence for torts toward third parties, lost if selection was negligent or the act authorised | Mostly the subcontractor's. Middleman at risk if it chose negligently or directed the work |
| Consumer-law duties (14ג disclosure, cancellation, price, identity) | All on the middleman for every sale | Mainly on the subcontractor for the service contract. The middleman's ads and site still subject to s.2, price and review rules |
| Disclaimer of liability in terms | Presumptively unfair (s.4(1)), likely void | Defensible if the structure is real and disclosed up front |
| Misleading-presentation risk | Low if it truly delivers as the company and does not misdescribe its staff | High if branded as "the company that does the work" while the customer actually contracts with someone else |
| Privacy | Controller of the customer database. Subcontractors are outsourced processors (reg. 15 agreements) | Risk of "data broker" status (registration above 10,000 people; medium security) if lead-selling is the main purpose |
| Spam | Middleman sends marketing, so it needs consent or the 30א(c) exception | Same for the broker's own marketing. Subcontractors need their own consent to market |
| Small claims | Customer sues the middleman (up to NIS 39,900) | Customer sues the subcontractor, often the broker too |
| Pricing, margin, brand control | Full | Weaker, since the subcontractor sets or owns the contract price. The margin is a referral fee |
| Look and feel | Can honestly be "a service company" | Must look like a "network or platform" to avoid misleading |

- Hybrid to consider (inference): run the general-contractor model and manage its risk through (1) written back-to-back subcontracts with indemnity, warranty and insurance requirements (liability insurance is for the insurance researcher); (2) documented vetting of each subcontractor, which defends against s.15(1) negligent selection; (3) a compliant distance-sale disclosure document, VAT-inclusive pricing, and a no-start-before-cancellation-window policy; (4) honest "independent professionals" wording; (5) basic data-security and outsourcing agreements; (6) explicit spam consent on intake forms.
