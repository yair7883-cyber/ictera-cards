# The Israeli home-services market for a subcontracting "middleman" brand: demand, prices, margins, competition, lead costs and supply

Source note for the report writer: the fetch tool was blocked for midrag.co.il, ynet.co.il and gov.il in this session, so the figures below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Midrag figures are mostly self-reported "average price of real jobs" data from its users. Many other price sources are vendor or agency marketing pages. Treat every price as an indicative range, not an index. All money is in ₪. Where a source says whether VAT is included, that is noted. Years are given where the source gives them.

## 1. Demand: households with private gardens, and market size for gardening and landscaping

### Takeaway
I found no clean official count of Israeli households with a private garden. The best proxy is a CBS 2024 buildings figure reported in the press: about 39% of residential *buildings* are ground-attached (1–2 dwellings), out of about 2.96 million dwellings. Ground-attached construction has fallen to about 8.5–17% of new starts. The only market-size figures for gardening are about 20 years old: about ₪1.4–1.5 billion a year, 70% of it private consumers.

### Cited Findings
- Per the CBS buildings report for 2024 (as reported in the press), about 39% of buildings in Israel are ground-attached, meaning buildings with 1–2 dwellings: private houses, villas, garden or roof apartments and semi-detached houses. In mid-2024 Israel had about 2.96 million residential dwellings. The figure counts buildings, not dwellings, so it does not give the number of garden households. — [ice.co.il](https://www.ice.co.il/realestate/news/article/1048210) (search extract; the page was not opened to confirm the exact wording)
- Construction starts of ground-attached units: 15,582 units in 2010 (about 40% of starts); about 8,000 in 2020 (17% of starts); 4,138 in H1 2021. — [Nadlan Center](https://www.nadlancenter.co.il/article/5181); [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/real-estate/article/hkilucmbf)
- Headline: "for every 1,000 apartments built in Israel, only 85 are built on the ground" (8.5%). — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/ryzbm7dwlx)
- Sources disagree on the 2019 count of ground-attached starts: 5,990 in one and 10,803 in another, probably because of different definitions. — [Nadlan Center](https://www.nadlancenter.co.il/article/5181); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/s1sbx6ke3)
- Gardening market size (2005 survey by "Mashov"): about ₪1.4 billion a year in gardening and garden infrastructure. The head of the Gardening and Landscape Organization estimated ₪1.5 billion. 70% of spending is by private consumers and 30% by institutions such as local authorities and businesses. The average household spends close to ₪1,000 a year on gardening. — [Globes (2005)](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=862842); [Kan Israel](https://kanisrael.co.il/5915/)
- Number of gardeners (2005): Mashov counted 4,800 active gardeners; the Gardening and Landscape Organization said 8,000–10,000. Total employment in the sector, including nurseries, gardening firms and municipalities, was about 20,000. The sector has "a very low barrier to entry". — [Globes (2005)](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=862842); [Kan Israel](https://kanisrael.co.il/5915/)
- A commercial fertiliser site claims Israel has "more than 2 million balconies", a possible market for balcony gardens. This is a commercial, unverified claim. — [Deshanit](https://www.deshanit.co.il/%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%9E-2-%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C-%D7%94%D7%96%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA/)

### Inferences
- Estimate: if roughly a quarter to a third of the ~2.96M dwellings are in ground-attached buildings or are garden apartments, the addressable "own a garden" base is on the order of 0.7–1.0M households. This is an unsourced order-of-magnitude estimate, derived from the 39%-of-buildings figure. That figure overstates the dwelling share, because each apartment block holds many dwellings. Garden apartments in blocks add to the base.
- New supply of private gardens is shrinking (8.5–17% of starts), so garden demand rests on the existing housing stock (moshavim, suburbs, Sharon, Shfela) rather than on growth.
- The ₪1.4–1.5B (2005) market figure is too old to use without adjustment, but it shows that private households dominate demand (70%). That suits a consumer-facing brand.

### Gaps
- No current CBS table of dwellings by building type was retrievable (gov.il and cbs.gov.il were not reachable in this session). The report writer should treat the garden-household count as an estimate.
- No current (2020s) Israeli gardening or landscaping market-size figure was found. Statista and Technavio have paid Israel data that was not accessible.

## 2. Water hardness by region, softener demand, players, prices and technologies

### Takeaway
Israeli tap water is a regional mix of very soft desalinated water (about 20–80 mg/L) and hard well or aquifer water (often 300+ mg/L). Measured city averages in the centre are moderate: Holon about 138 mg/L, Or Yehuda about 111, Tel Aviv generally above 150. Some cities have reported peaks of 330–390 mg/L. The mass market is drinking-water bars (Strauss Water/Tami4, about 929K service contracts; Electra re-entering). Whole-house salt softeners are a niche for private houses at about ₪6,400–10,600 installed, alongside cheaper polyphosphate or electronic "scale inhibitors" at about ₪1,180–1,450 installed.

### Cited Findings
**Hardness and desalination**
- Classification: 0–60 mg/L CaCO3 is soft, 60–120 moderate, above 120 hard and above 180 very hard. Israel has no binding hardness standard, because calcium and magnesium are not harmful to health; the problem is scale. — [maim.co.il](https://www.maim.co.il/article.aspx?id=17193); [Meiraveh](https://meiraveh.co.il/water-hardness-levels-by-region/)
- Tel Aviv: the water utility says the city's water is generally above 150 mg/L, which is hard to very hard. — [Mei Avivim](https://www.mei-avivim.co.il/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D2/%D7%A7%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- Holon has an annual average of about 138 mg/L and Or Yehuda about 111 mg/L. — [Mei Shikma water utility](https://mei-shikma.co.il/engineering-%D7%A7%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- A commercial table of the highest values found by city gives Nof HaGalil/Nazareth Illit 390, Bnei Brak 385, Haifa 366, Raanana 347 and Beersheba 329. These are peak values, units are not stated (probably mg/L CaCO3) and no measurement date is given. — [maim.co.il, "hard water by region 2026"](https://www.maim.co.il/article.aspx?id=17193)
- Commercial filter sellers name the Shfela and the centre (Petah Tikva, Rehovot, Ness Ziona), the south (Beersheba area), Samaria and the Jordan Valley as very hard (300–500+ mg/L), and the Galilee as moderate. They say Jerusalem's water from the mountain aquifer is especially hard. These are vendor claims and conflict with the utility averages above. — [Meiraveh](https://meiraveh.co.il/water-hardness-levels-by-region/); [Plagim](https://www.plagim-water.co.il/knowledge-center/%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%A7%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C-%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%9B%D7%9C-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%9C%D7%93%D7%90%D7%95%D7%92/)
- Desalinated water is very soft (about 20–80 mg/L). Scale comes from well water, and the mix changes by region and season. — [Tamhil (water lab)](https://tamhil.com/%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C/)
- Desalinated share of drinking water, with inconsistent definitions: about 60% of drinking water (a 2025 water-sector report as cited by a commercial site); about 70% of households served (Taub Center, as cited); about 75% of municipal water (2025 article); "about 80%" (a water lab citing the Water Authority). — [Tamhil](https://tamhil.com/%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C/); [Israel Asakim (2025)](https://www.israelasakim.co.il/israel-desalination-business-infrastructure-risk/); [Eden Bar](https://edenbar.co.il/%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D/%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%9C-%D7%91%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA-2025-%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%94-2/)
- The Ministry of Health 2023 survey of magnesium and calcium in drinking-water systems reports regional differences driven by desalination share. The extracted text was garbled, so no reliable regional numbers could be taken from it. — [Ministry of Health survey 2023 (PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/magnesium-calcium-drinking-water-supply-systems-survey-2023/he/files_publications_units_environmental_health_Magnesium-calcium-drinking-water-supply-systems-2023-survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- Technologies Israelis buy, ranked by penetration and inferred from the sources: (1) filtered or chilled water bars on a service contract (Tami4, about 929K contracts; Electra); (2) under-sink or main-line polyphosphate or electronic scale inhibitors at about ₪1,200–1,500; (3) whole-house ion-exchange salt softeners at about ₪6,400–10,600, mainly in private houses. Magnetic devices are sold but do not soften.
- Softener demand is limited by geography. The coastal centre gets a large desalinated share, so averages there are moderate (111–150 mg/L). The strongest pitch is in private houses in very hard well-water areas (Shfela, Sharon towns such as Raanana, the south, the Galilee hills, Jerusalem and the West Bank), and these overlap with the garden-owning base.
- Margin pressure for a middleman: softener vendors already publish prices that include installation (₪6,400–7,400 all-in), so a reseller brand has little room to mark up the same hardware unless it buys from importers at trade prices. Trade (wholesale) prices were not found.
- Softeners add a recurring revenue stream: salt (about ₪30 per bag), resin service, and inhibitor cartridges (₪176–211 every few months). Water bars show that Israelis accept service subscriptions.

### Gaps
- No official Water Authority or Mekorot table of hardness by region could be read (gov.il was blocked). City data covers only Tel Aviv, Holon, Or Yehuda and a vendor's peak table.
- No penetration figure for whole-house softeners in Israeli homes, and no unit-sales data.
- No importer or wholesale prices for softener hardware, which are needed to compute the middleman margin.

## 3. Prices in Israel (2025–2026, ₪)

### Takeaway
Typical retail ranges: garden maintenance ₪200–400 per visit, or about ₪400–900 a month for gardens up to about 100 m². One-time garden clean-up about ₪1,065 on average (Midrag, May 2025). Irrigation systems ₪1,500–6,000. Artificial grass ₪140–250/m² installed (real 40–60 m² jobs averaged about ₪9,200–9,500). Aluminium pergolas ₪700–1,800/m² excl. VAT. Decks ₪300–1,300/m² by material (composite ₪600–650/m²). Tree pruning ₪600–1,600. Whole-house softeners ₪6,400–10,600 installed. Scale inhibitors and under-sink units about ₪1,000–1,450.

## 4. Margins: what Israeli contractors keep when subcontracting, and what subcontractors charge

### Takeaway
No vertical-specific Israeli data was found. General contracting benchmarks: a "contractor's profit" of about 15% on subcontracted work is treated as reasonable and customary in at least one court ruling. Renovation contractors target about 25–40% gross margin and 5–12% net. Midrag, the main lead platform, takes a 5–10% commission per closed job, which also shows that installers accept giving up about 10%.

### Cited Findings
- Gross margins: apartment renovations about 25–40%, multi-unit residential construction 15–25%, large projects 10–18%. Net margins of 5–12% are "common and healthy"; below 5% signals a pricing or cost problem. — [Horizon (contractor CFO guide)](https://horizon.org.il/cfo-outsource/financial-management-contractors-israel/)
- In a dispute between a main contractor and a subcontractor, a court expert set a 15% "contractor's profit" deduction as reasonable and customary in the industry. The court adopted it; the other side argued for 20%. — [Fridman law office summary](https://www.fridmanwork.com/LAWx-lawyers127521.html)
- The Dekel construction price database (the standard Israeli cost book) adds a defined "main contractor" percentage to subcontractor prices, set per work chapter. — [Dekel database extract (PDF, Modiin regional council)](https://modiin-region-public.s3.il-central-1.amazonaws.com/uploads/n/1567594749.8638.pdf)
- Solo renovators are cited with margins up to about 50%. — [PowerDrilling](https://www.powerdrilling.co.il/%D7%9B%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%95%D7%99%D7%97-%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%9F-%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- Midrag charges professionals a 5–10% commission on closed and paid jobs, depending on the field. There is no fee for listing, leads or calls. A third-party site says it is typically about 10%, with a minimum fee per lead that led to a deal. — [Midrag for Business, fee calculator](https://biz.midrag.co.il/FeesCalculator); [Midrag: "how much does it cost to be rated"](https://biz.midrag.co.il/Midragonim/Answer/18572); [ToSuccess](https://tosuccess.co.il/%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%92-%D7%95%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%A0%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D/)
- Subcontractor and labour cost references for gardening:
  - Salaried gardeners earn about ₪7,000–12,000 a month (average posted salary about ₪10,000); experienced ones with a pest-control licence earn ₪9,000–12,000. Self-employed gardeners reach about ₪18,000 a month. — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/career/news/article/20039747)
  - Minimum wage: ₪34.33/hour and ₪6,443.85/month from April 2025 to March 2026, with a newer rate of ₪35.40/hour noted. — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A9%D7%9B%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9D)

### Inferences
These are estimates for unit economics, not sourced figures.
- A middleman who sells at market retail and pays the installer the installer's own retail-minus price can realistically expect about 15–30% gross margin per job. 15% is the court-accepted "contractor's profit"; 25–40% is the renovation gross-margin range. Margins above 30% are plausible only where retail prices are spread widely (artificial grass, pergolas) and the brand sells at the top of the range.
- A Midrag-like 10% fee is the "outside option" for installers. A middleman who keeps much more than 10–15% will have to give installers something extra: a steady flow of jobs, prompt payment, and no selling time.
- Garden maintenance example (estimate): on a ₪600-a-month client, a 20–30% margin leaves about ₪120–180 a month per client. Hundreds of active clients would be needed for a meaningful income.
- High-ticket installs example (estimate): a ₪20,000 pergola at 20% yields about ₪4,000 a job.

### Gaps
- No Israeli source gives what gardening companies or artificial-grass and pergola firms actually pay their subcontractors (per m², per visit or per day). Installer-side rates for artificial grass, pergola, deck or softener installation were not found.
- No wholesale price for artificial grass rolls, pergola profiles or softener units, which a material-plus-labour model needs.

## 6. Supply of subcontractors: availability, rates, reliability, workforce and payment practices

### Takeaway
Gardening has a very low barrier to entry and thousands of practitioners: 4,800–10,000 gardeners (2005). Gardeners are reachable, but no gardening-specific workforce or reliability data was found. There is a voluntary registry, the Israeli Gardeners Association. The wider labour context since October 2023 is a severe shortage of Palestinian and foreign workers in construction and agriculture, which probably tightens supply for installation trades. Payment practices (cash or invoices) are not documented for gardening, beyond consumer-protection guidance that refusing an invoice is a complaint issue.

### Cited Findings
- 4,800 active gardeners (Mashov survey, 2005) versus 8,000–10,000 (Gardening and Landscape Organization). The sector has "a very low barrier to entry". — [Globes (2005)](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=862842)
- The Israeli Gardeners Association calls itself a regulatory public body that registers and oversees people working in gardening, planting and garden maintenance. It handles disputes between registered gardeners and clients; registration is voluntary. — [Gardening.org.il](https://gardening.org.il/israeli-gardeners-association/)
- Gardener earnings: salaried ₪7,000–12,000 a month; self-employed up to about ₪18,000 a month. — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/career/news/article/20039747)
- Consumer-side problem documented: households get fined by municipalities when a gardener dumps garden waste in the wrong place or on the wrong day. Ynet advises agreeing in writing who pays such fines. — [ynet consumer article](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4915821,00.html)
- Refusing to issue an invoice is listed among the issues the Consumer Protection Authority handles. — [Shakoof](https://shakoof.org.il/%D7%AA%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94-%D7%9C%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9C%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%9F-%D7%9E%D7%AA%D7%99-%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93-%D7%95%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9A-%D7%9E%D7%92%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9D)
- Labour context: construction lacked about 38,000 workers as of Q1 2025 (Ministry of Finance chief economist). After the war began, only about 6,000 Palestinian workers were allowed into "essential" workplaces, and foreign-worker quotas for construction were raised. These figures are not gardening-specific, and some are from 2023–2024. — [Nadlan Center](https://www.nadlancenter.co.il/article/12347); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/skzwajyza); [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001461063)
- An older (2000s) ynet piece describes the gardening market as "completely unregulated" ("פרוץ לחלוטין"). — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-2688596,00.html)

### Inferences
- Gardeners are numerous and fragmented, so recruiting subcontractors for maintenance is likely easy. Quality control and no-shows are the main risk (inference; no Israeli survey found).
- For installation trades (artificial grass, pergolas, decks, waterproofing), the post-2023 labour shortage in construction-adjacent trades probably raises installer prices and lowers availability. That squeezes the middleman's margin (inference).
- Artificial-grass and softener installers that already run their own branded companies may be reluctant to work as white-label subcontractors for a competitor brand. Independent crews and part-time installers are the likely pool (inference).

### Gaps
- No data on the workforce composition of Israeli gardeners (Jewish Israeli, Arab Israeli, Palestinian, foreign workers), on the share paid in cash, or on how often gardeners issue invoices. Practitioner Facebook-group discussions were not retrievable.
- No survey of no-show or complaint rates by trade.
- No data on subcontracting day rates for installation crews.

## 7. Seasonality by vertical

### Takeaway
Almost no sourced Israeli seasonality data was found. Midrag recommends spring (March–May) for lawn fertilising. Everything else below is inference from Israel's climate (dry summer May–October, rainy winter November–March) and must be labelled as such.

### Cited Findings
- Natural-lawn fertilising is recommended in spring (March–May), when growth resumes after winter. — [Midrag tip](https://www.midrag.co.il/Content/Tip/161)
- Artificial grass in a private garden needs cleaning about every 2 months, with deep maintenance only in the transition seasons. — [Pro.co.il](https://pro.co.il/gardners/guide/how-to-clean-synthetic)

### Inferences
These are climate-based estimates, not sourced.
- Garden maintenance: demand all year, peaking in spring and summer (growth and irrigation), lower in winter, with some clients pausing. Churn risk is highest in winter.
- Garden development and artificial grass: likely peaks in spring and early summer, before outdoor season and holidays, and in early autumn, before the holidays. Winter rain makes groundwork harder.
- Pergolas and decks: spring and summer buying for outdoor living; possibly a pre-winter wave for roofed pergolas against rain.
- Waterproofing: a strong pre-winter peak (August–November) and emergency demand after the first rains.
- AC cleaning: pre-summer (April–June), with a smaller pre-winter heating peak.
- Softeners and filters: no strong seasonality expected; scale complaints are all year.

### Gaps
- No Google Trends or platform data on seasonal demand for these terms was retrieved.

- Many service sites target "[service] + city" pages, such as gardener in Gedera or gardener in Bnei Brak. This confirms that city-modified local search is a live acquisition channel in Israel, as in the US model (inference).

- Results pages for price queries such as "מחיר דשא סינטטי" (artificial grass price) are crowded with Midrag, B144, Pro.co.il and vendor price lists. Ranking organically for these terms will be competitive (inference).

## 9. Ranking of candidate verticals for a subcontracting "middleman" brand in Israel

### Takeaway
On the sourced numbers, the strongest fits are mid-to-high-ticket, standardised outdoor installs that are priced per m² with wide price spreads: (1) artificial grass and (2) pergolas and decks. Garden maintenance works only as a volume or recurring add-on. Whole-house softeners are a narrow niche facing transparent vendor pricing. Waterproofing has a high ticket but very high defect and liability risk. AC cleaning and solar water heaters are low-ticket or technical. This ranking is an analytic judgement (inference) built on the cited figures above.

Notes on the ranking (inference):
- The US playbook (local SEO for "landscaping") maps best to Israel's artificial-grass and pergola niches. Those have ₪7,000–36,000 tickets where a ₪100 lead and a 20% margin work. Monthly gardening at ₪400–900 is a much smaller ticket than US landscaping projects, and gardening is fragmented and cheap.
- The garden-owning base is a minority of Israeli housing and is shrinking in new construction (section 1). That caps total demand compared with US suburbs.
- Softeners look weaker in Israel than in the US model: desalination supplies much of the water (about 60–80% by various definitions), vendors already publish installed prices of ₪6,400–7,400, and the mass market is drinking-water bars. Softeners are better as a cross-sell to garden or house clients in hard-water areas than as a standalone brand.
- Waterproofing has an attractive ticket, but defect risk is high. A middleman without trade expertise carries the warranty exposure.
